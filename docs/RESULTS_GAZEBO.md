# Controller comparison in Gazebo

Generated by `analysis/compare_controllers.py` from `results/gazebo/metrics.csv`. Do not edit by hand: `scripts/run_gazebo_repeats.sh` regenerates the whole file.

> These are physics-simulation runs, scored against the Gazebo ground-truth pose. They are **not** the controller comparison: the runs use a static `map -> odom` with AMCL disabled, so the error is dominated by odometry drift rather than by the control law, and Gazebo runs are not bit-reproducible. See [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md) for the controller comparison proper.

## Conclusion

Too few scenarios here to draw a conclusion: on `corner` Pure Pursuit was closer (0.0731 m against 0.0759 m RMS). This is a single-route check, not the controller comparison; see docs/RESULTS.md for that, which covers seven scenarios at three speeds with and without odometry noise.

## How the comparison is kept fair

- Both controllers drive the **same** reference paths from the **same** start poses at the **same** speeds.
- The longitudinal (speed) law is shared code. Only the lateral control law differs, so a difference in tracking error cannot be explained by one controller simply taking corners slower.
- Cross-track error is reported at `base_link` for both, even though Stanley internally regulates the error at a virtual front axle.
- Errors are measured against **ground truth**, not against the pose the controller was given.
- Both controllers face the same world, the same latched plan and the same start pose. Sensor noise and sensing latency are whatever the simulator and the ROS transport produce, not a model, and are therefore **not** identical between runs.
- Gains are the ones fitted offline; nothing is tuned here.

## Reading these numbers honestly

- These are runs in Gazebo (gz-sim) against its physics engine, scored against the simulator's ground-truth pose. Wheel odometry is integrated from the wheel joints, so the controller drives on a dead-reckoned estimate that drifts.
- Where a route was repeated, each table row is the mean over the repeats and the figures show the last repeat. Every repeat is listed in `results/controller_comparison.json`.
- A difference between the two controllers smaller than the spread between repeats of the same controller is not a result.
